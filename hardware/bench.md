# Bench checkout · Rev. 0.4 hub circuit and Rev. 0.6 power paths

**PROPOSED.** Use this procedure with the four front accelerometer leads (W101–W104), both microphone leads (W201–W202) and the bench supply. Record which drawing revision and circuit variant you built. Vehicle fuse taps, cable routing and mounting are separate installation decisions.

## Inspect the disconnected cables and power up one hub

1. Orient the ESP32-WROOM-32 carrier component side up with USB at the bottom. Compare its two 15-pin headers with the drawing. VIN is at the bottom left, 3V3 at the bottom right, and GND immediately above each. Photograph the markings and connector mating faces. Identify both sensor breakouts and confirm their supply requirements, fitted pull resistors, level shifters and decoupling. [ESP32 header map](esp32-header.md)
2. Disconnect both ends of each cable. Check continuity on each conductor individually, orientation at each connector, and isolation between conductors and shield. Checking loose cables first catches a broken return that the parallel grounds could mask after assembly.
3. For J1–J4, verify pins **2 SCK, 4 CS, 6 SDIO, 8 +3.3 V**. Pins **1, 3, 5, 7** are separate returns joined at each end. Verify pairs **1–2, 3–6, 4–5, 7–8**. The accelerometer shield terminates at the hub only. Confirm the chosen hub shield terminal and mark passive 8P8C ports **CUSTOM SENSOR — NON-ETHERNET**.
4. For J5–J6, verify **1 OUT, 2 signal return, 3 +3.3 V, 4 power return**, with 1–2 and 3–4 as pairs. Check the two returns individually before joining them. Microphone shield termination is TBD. Record the actual bench arrangement.
5. Verify P01 polarity from source to hub (red positive, black return) and check for an unintended supply-to-ground short. Identify the carrier regulator and confirm the VIN input range, available 3.3 V output current and isolation between VIN and USB power. With USB and sensors disconnected, apply a current-limited supply to the verified hub input. Measure the nominal 5 V at VIN and 3.3 V at the far sensor connectors. Power down to connect each instrument, then repeat under load with Wi-Fi active. Compare voltage minima and peak current with the actual boards' operating limits.

## Qualify the two vehicle power paths on the bench

Use the [Mini-Box and Pi wiring](wiring/generated/pi-power.svg), [HK4 supply wiring](wiring/generated/hk4-hub-power.svg) and [PS1 hub branches](wiring/generated/power.svg) to locate the measurement points.

1. **VIOFO HK4 → hub branches P01–P03.** Trace the USB-C adapter's feed pair to PS1 5V-IN and RTN-IN. Verify polarity at each branch and its positive-side fuse before connecting a hub. Record total HK4 output current and minimum VIN at each hub during simultaneous startup and Wi-Fi transmission. Compare total load with the HK4's **5 V, 2 A combined** rating. With its battery feed maintained, remove the ACC signal and observe output and idle current. The kit uses ACC to signal parking mode to a VIOFO camera and can continue supplying power after key-off. Check the selected low-voltage cutoff with a variable bench supply. [HK4 manual, p. 2](<docs/HK4 Hardwire Kit English Manual.pdf#page=2>)
2. **Mini-Box output voltage, Pi disconnected.** The right-hand Mini-Fit output defaults to **12 V**. Identify the board revision, select 5 V, power-cycle, then meter polarity and voltage at the intended USB-C plug. Connect a 3 A dummy load through the finished lead and verify 5 V at the plug under load. The Mini-Box mini-USB port is for configuration. [Mini-Box guide, pp. 2–3](docs/PWR-DCDC-USB-manual.pdf#page=2)
3. **Mini-Box key-off timing, dummy load still attached.** Remove input power and select automotive mode and a timing scheme with a positive, finite HARDOFF. The guide lists both immediate cutoff and settings that keep power on indefinitely. Restore power with IGN on. Maintain the battery feed, switch IGN off and time IGN loss → PSW pulse (OFFDELAY), then PSW pulse → loss of 5 V at the plug (HARDOFF). Restore IGN and verify the output returns. Record the selected scheme, battery voltage and both measured intervals. Below 11.2 V during HARDOFF the converter can cut power early. PSW is a motherboard power-button interface and requires a separate Pi-compatible interface. [Mini-Box timing table, p. 4](docs/PWR-DCDC-USB-manual.pdf#page=4)
4. **Pi and SSD shutdown.** On a steady 5 V Pi supply, exercise the selected shutdown interface while recording to the SSD. Time the shutdown request through final file close and completed OS halt. Proceed to automatic cutoff only when the measured window leaves margin beyond that shutdown time. Power the recorder from the qualified Mini-Box lead, check voltage at the Pi during SSD startup and writes, then repeat key-off. Verify halt precedes loss of 5 V and reopen the recording after restart.
5. **Both supplies on one ignition cycle.** Maintain the simulated battery feed and switch both ACC/IGN sense inputs off together. Record the last hub sample received by the Pi, any buffered samples or reported gaps, OS halt and the time each rail falls. The HK4 can keep the hubs transmitting after the Pi stops. Measure total parked input current in that state. Restore ACC/IGN and check that the Pi boots, the hubs reconnect and the next recording accounts for the gap.
6. **Engine cranking.** Capture a representative Civic start, or replay its measured supply dip and switched-sense timing on the bench. With the recorder and hubs active before cranking, trace voltage at both converter inputs, HK4 ACC and Mini-Box IGN, the Pi USB-C plug, and VIN at the end of P03. Record minima and dip durations, Pi undervoltage or reset events, hub resets, and sample counters across the start. Continuous recording passes when both load rails stay within the loads' verified limits, the devices stay up and the recording spans the start without a gap. If a device resets, check the file, time reconnection and identify the missing samples. Report that result as recovery after an interruption.

## Compare fitted parts with the drawings

Use the connector numbers on the harness drawing and the labels on the physical breakout. The vendor pin numbers below locate the IC signals when tracing the breakout.

- **ADXL345.** The IC clock is pin 14, three-wire data pin 13, chip select pin 7 and four-wire SDO pin 12. Trace the board's power path to VS (pin 6, 2.0–3.6 V) and VDD I/O (pin 1, 1.7 V up to VS). Confirm the logic rail and any level shifter support the hub's bidirectional 3.3 V data line. Check existing bypassing at both rails before fitting the proposed capacitors. [Pin table](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adxl345.pdf#page=6) · [Electrical ratings](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adxl345.pdf#page=4) · [Supply bypassing](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adxl345.pdf#page=29)
- **MAX4466.** On the five-pin IC, OUT is pin 4, GND pin 2 and VCC pin 5. Its operating range is 2.4–5.5 V. The minimum stable amplifier gain is 5 V/V. The breakout's feedback network sets its actual gain, and the hub's 0.680 divider scales its output separately. Trace the breakout's header and measure its quiet output bias before using the ideal ADC bias below. [IC pinout and gain](docs/MAX4465-MAX4469.pdf#page=1) · [Example preamp circuit](docs/MAX4465-MAX4469.pdf#page=8)

## Bring up each accelerometer after power-up

For the initial short **four-wire** check, take breakout SDO to GPIO19 on a separate jumper. Select the port and read `DEVID` (0x00) over MISO. Expect `0xE5`. The scheduled eight-contact cable carries bidirectional SDIO on pin 6 and uses the full contact set. Power down before exchanging leads.

For **three-wire** checkout, run the sequence below for each sensor at every power-up, including after changing to the full-length cable. At reset the sensor uses four-wire SPI, 100 Hz and standby. Keep all chip selects high while setting SPI mode 3 (clock idle high) at 1 MHz. On the switched build, select and enable the intended U2 channel before asserting that port's CS. Use a separate CS transaction for each register access. [Serial interface](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adxl345.pdf#page=14) · [Reset register map](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adxl345.pdf#page=23)

| Order | Register | Bench setting and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `DATA_FORMAT` (0x31) | First **write** `0x4B` on GPIO23/SDIO. This selects three-wire SPI, full resolution and ±16 g. For reads, drive the command then release SDIO for the sensor's reply. Read back `0x4B` and check `DEVID = 0xE5` on the same wire. |
| 2 | `BW_RATE` (0x2C) | Write and read back `0x0D` for 800 Hz, normal power. |
| 3 | `POWER_CTL` (0x2D) | Write and read back `0x08` to enter measurement mode. Verify changing axis data when rotating the breakout. |

## Compare the switched and direct SPI builds

The switched schematic routes both signals through U2. Removing U2 opens SCK and SDIO to every port. For the **direct-bus bench variant**, disconnect power, leave U2 unpopulated, and strap its numbered terminals as drawn. Each branch keeps its series resistor or bench link and its independent chip select on port pin 4. Remove all eight straps before fitting U2 for the switched build. [Direct-bus strap drawing](schematic/direct-bus.svg) · [Switched hub schematic](schematic/standard-hub.pdf)

Start with short leads. On the direct bus, inactive ADXL345s see clock and data traffic with CS high and can interpret it as I²C. To check for unintended responses, write a different temporary X offset (`OFSX`, 0x1E) to each selected sensor and read them all back after cycling through the other ports. Restore each original offset and verify its readback. Use a logic analyzer to check CS, SCK and SDIO for unexpected acknowledgments during traffic to another port. Investigate any mismatches or unexpected replies before extending the leads. [ADXL345 bus-traffic guidance](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/adxl345.pdf#page=14)

## Exercise the acquisition paths

1. **One accelerometer.** Apply the startup sequence first on a short lead and again after powering up on a full-length lead. Rotate the sensor through each axis and verify the gravity response and recorded orientation.
2. **Four accelerometers.** Populate the 8, 8, 5 and 5 ft proposed branches. Move each sensor in turn to verify its port identity. With both microphones active and Wi-Fi streaming, record run duration, per-channel sample counters, FIFO overruns, missing blocks and resets. Aim for zero overruns, unaccounted gaps and resets during a declared uninterrupted run. Interrupt Wi-Fi once and verify that any gap after reconnection is accounted for. Compare direct and mux wiring as separate builds before selecting one.
3. **Microphone response.** Save native 32 kHz/channel counts with settings fixed. Compare a quiet input and a reference tone with Wi-Fi idle and streaming. Record noise RMS in ADC counts, tone frequency and amplitude, clipping count, and ADC attenuation setting. Compare the native and digitally filtered 16 kHz versions. Define the required passband, noise floor and allowable aliasing for the intended measurement before claiming validation.
4. **Relative timing.** Mount two accelerometers on the same rigid block with axes aligned. Apply a repeatable vibration at the start and end of the run. Estimate channel offset and drift from that common input and report the uncertainty. Repeat across hubs when a second hub is available. Choose the timing limit from the intended measurement. For example, 10° phase error at 200 Hz allows about 139 µs of relative error.

Keep raw samples, scope traces and run notes together in `captures/imports/<bench-run-id>/`. Record the date, drawing revision, instrument and cable IDs, measured lengths, fitted parts, acquisition settings and reference instruments. Include the CarDAQ capture ID if OBD was recorded concurrently. Mark values **MEASURED** when backed by a trace and **VALIDATED** when they meet a stated criterion for that configuration.
